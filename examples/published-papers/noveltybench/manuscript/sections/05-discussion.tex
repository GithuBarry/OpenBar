\section{Discussion and Future Work}
\label{sec-discussion}
% OB-EDIT-BEGIN openbarry/markdown@1 id=discussion
Our findings suggest that language models lack distributional diversity; given a fixed prompt, their outputs are heavily skewed toward certain responses over other equally valid ones.
Users can to some extent elicit novel generations by modifying their prompt or iteratively asking ``give me a different answer.''
These workarounds are cumbersome and do not address the fundamental issue that modern language models output overly low-entropy distributions.

One key question our benchmark does not address is the extent to which users \emph{want} diversity.
For the dice-rolling prompt the need is obvious.
For the car recommendation example it may be desirable for the LLM to give a consistent response.
Should an LLM's generations represent a diverse group of hypothetical humans, or should they possess fixed, consistent behavior?
Consistency is crucial for high-stakes applications; it can conversely lead to the LLM imposing a single view on users.

Maximum likelihood estimation inherently optimizes language models toward a single response for each prompt.
This, combined with alignment algorithms which often associate a prompt with a single high-reward response, unsurprisingly causes language models to favor a certain response among several reasonable choices~\cite{zhang2024diverging}.
Recent work has begun addressing this limitation through modified training objectives during supervised fine-tuning~\cite{li2024preserving} and preference optimization~\cite{lanchantin2025}, but these approaches represent only initial steps.
